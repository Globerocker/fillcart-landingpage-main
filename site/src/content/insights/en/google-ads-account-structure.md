## The Core Idea: Structure Beats Chaos

Too many Google Ads accounts suffer from **campaign inflation**. A separate campaign is set up for every new idea, every feature or every target group. That may look tidy at first glance, but in the long run it leads to an overloaded account, data that is hard to maintain and inefficient use of budget.

**My key learning:** Keep it lean. Plan big at the campaign level, think granular at the ad group level.

## Example: How My Campaign Planner Is Structured

In my Excel template I work with the following columns:

- Target region
- Product or feature
- Campaign type (Search, PMax, Display, etc.)
- Target URL
- Budget guideline
- Dynamic campaign name generation via formula

Especially in teams with approval processes, Excel or Google Sheets is unbeatable. It offers versioning, comments, live access and automated validations (character counters, mandatory fields, etc.).

## Campaign Setup by Example

### Target markets

- Germany and Switzerland (DE)
- Poland (PL)
- Spain (ES)

### Campaign types

- Search ads
- Performance Max (PMax)
- Display ads (for example whitepaper campaigns)

### Example structure

| Country | Campaign | Ad group | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DE/CH | `Search_DE_Legal` | Legal Time Tracking | Keywords + landing page |
| PL | `Search_PL_Konkurrenz` | Brand + support combos | Competitor and problem keywords |
| ES | `PMAX_ES_Legal` | automatic | Compare content with Search |

Running Search and PMax in parallel lets you decide based on data which campaign type converts better, instead of just estimating from experience.

## Keyword Research: Semrush, ChatGPT and Co.

Without strong keyword clusters there is no clean structure. Here is my tool selection:

- **Semrush:** Keyword Magic Tool, SERPs, competitors
- **ChatGPT:** keyword ideas, translations, ad copy
- **Google Keyword Planner:** search volume and CPC
- **AlsoAsked / AnswerThePublic:** topic clusters and questions

My process:

1. Choose a seed keyword ("Legal Case Management")
2. Start the Semrush analysis
3. Check the top SERPs and ads
4. Define keyword clusters
5. One cluster = one ad group

## Writing Ad Copy With a System

My proven structure:

| Type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | Legal processes take too long? |
| USP | Save time with a smart case overview |
| CTA | Start your trial now |

I create:

- 15 headlines (max. 30 characters)
- 4 descriptions (max. 90 characters)
- Long headlines for PMax

Character counters and GPT are my friends, but I always make the final selection myself.

## Content Workflow and Approval Process

The more people are involved, the more important clarity becomes. A central sheet with note fields, comments and versioning is worth its weight in gold.

Especially for multilingual campaigns: keep each language sheet separate, don't mix them!

## Learnings From Campaign Planning

- Less is more: ad groups instead of feature campaigns
- Countries, not just languages: DE ≠ CH ≠ AT
- No feature cluster without a landing page!
- GPT saves time but does not replace your brain
- PMax vs. Search: a permanent battle with a learning effect

## Conclusion

If you want to scale Google Ads, you have to **structure instead of tinker**. With a well thought-out Excel structure, clever keyword research and a clean content workflow, you can massively accelerate your setup and achieve better results.

Tools like ChatGPT or Semrush are not magic, they are tools. The strategy behind them decides whether your budget works or fizzles out.
