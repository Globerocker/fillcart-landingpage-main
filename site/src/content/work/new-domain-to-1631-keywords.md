## Context

A European legal-tech software group brought several products under one new brand and one new domain. The old sites had rankings that paid for themselves every month. The new domain had none. The board wanted the brand change; nobody wanted to lose the search traffic that came with the old names.

## The problem

A domain change is where rankings go to die. Every URL changes, internal links break, canonicals point to the wrong place, and Google has to relearn a site it already trusted. Most relaunches lose traffic for months. On top of that, the new domain started at 29 ranking keywords, so growth had to come on top of a migration that could not fail.

## What I did

- Built the URL map first: every old URL to its new target, with the redirect type, the canonical and the structural reason.
- Planned the new site structure around topics the buyers search for, not around the org chart.
- Ran pre launch checks: redirects, canonicals, indexing rules, internal links, structured data.
- Supported the go live and monitored rankings and traffic daily for four weeks, fixing what moved.
- Kept the content engine running on the new domain so growth came from new pages, not only from migrated ones.

## What was built

One domain with a topic based structure, a complete redirect map from the old domains, consistent canonicals and a monitoring setup in Google Search Console and GA4 that compares migrated pages before and after the move. The content runs on HubSpot CMS, so new pages land in the same lifecycle and attribution as everything else.

## Results

The new domain went from 29 to 1,631 ranking keywords in twelve months, per rank tracking. It received 86,326 organic clicks in those twelve months, per Google Search Console. The migrated pages lost no traffic in the move, measured by comparing organic traffic on those pages before and after the redirects went live.
